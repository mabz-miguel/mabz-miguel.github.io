# ChatGPT Independent Audit — 01 Positioning

Status: first-pass independent audit. Claude's audit has not been read.

## Executive diagnosis

The current portfolio signals experience, ambition and a credible intersection of AI, digital production and workflow improvement. The main positioning problem is not lack of capability; it is **classification friction**.

The headline territory — **“AI-Driven Digital Production Systems”** — sounds sophisticated and differentiated, but it describes a field/approach more than a recognizable hiring category. In a fast recruiter scan, it can trigger: “interesting profile, but what exact role is this person?”

That distinction matters because recruiters often search and shortlist using role families before they investigate nuance.

## 30-second recruiter interpretation — current state

Likely interpretation:

> Senior digital-production/creative-technology professional with strong AI and automation orientation, experience around major brands, and some product/process thinking. Probably capable of improving workflows and bridging technical production with innovation, but the exact target role, leadership scope and business ownership are not immediately obvious.

Confidence: **high** for the ambiguity diagnosis; **medium** for the exact role interpretation until the CV and full LinkedIn are cross-checked.

## Strong signals already present

### P — Breadth is real, not cosmetic
The site presents AI, product thinking and production workflows as connected rather than as a list of unrelated tools. This is a good foundation for a senior narrative.

### P — Large-brand social proof
The portfolio visibly uses a “Clients & collaborations” section with recognizable brands such as Mercedes-Benz, IKEA, Heineken, Pfizer, Renault, Radisson, Repsol and others. This helps establish professional scale and credibility.

### P — The identity is not trapped in 3D execution
The current top-level language has already moved beyond a narrow CGI/3D specialist label. That is strategically useful and should be protected.

### P — The tone aims above individual-contributor craft
“AI-Driven Digital Production Systems” and the product/workflow framing imply system-level thinking. That is directionally compatible with innovation, delivery, program/project, product-operations or creative-technology leadership.

## Core positioning problems

### POS-P0-01 — No immediately recognizable hiring category
**Severity:** P0  
**Confidence:** high

**Evidence:** The leading identity is “AI-Driven Digital Production Systems.”

**Why it matters:** It tells the visitor the domain, but not whether Miguel should be shortlisted as a Project Manager, Product/Operations profile, Innovation Manager, Technical Program/Delivery Manager, Creative Technology lead, AI Transformation profile, or senior digital-production specialist.

**Recruiting consequence:** The recruiter must perform interpretation work. In a competitive funnel, that can reduce conversion even when the profile is strong.

**Direction:** Preserve the differentiated AI + production systems proposition, but anchor it to one primary recognizable job family and one secondary adjacent family after market validation.

---

### POS-P0-02 — Breadth currently risks reading as dispersion
**Severity:** P0  
**Confidence:** high

**Evidence:** The proposition combines AI, product thinking, production workflows, creative/digital production and multiple brand contexts.

**Why it matters:** Breadth becomes seniority only when the page explains the organizing principle: what Miguel owns, what decisions he makes, what outcomes he drives, and at what scale.

**Risk:** “He does many interesting things” instead of “he is the person who leads X kind of transformation.”

**Direction:** Build a hierarchy: primary professional identity → problems solved → evidence → selected capabilities. Do not present all capabilities as equal.

---

### POS-P0-03 — Leadership/ownership is not yet the dominant signal
**Severity:** P0  
**Confidence:** medium-high

**Evidence:** The current language strongly signals systems, tools and production, while leadership scope is not equally prominent in the first impression.

**Why it matters:** If the next move is toward manager/project/product/innovation responsibility, the site must prove not only that Miguel can build or improve things, but that he can define direction, coordinate people, make trade-offs, align stakeholders and own outcomes.

**Direction:** Every major project should explicitly expose role, ownership, decisions, stakeholders, constraints and outcome. Leadership should be demonstrated through evidence, not claimed with adjectives.

---

### POS-P1-04 — Business impact is less visible than technical sophistication
**Severity:** P1  
**Confidence:** medium-high

**Evidence:** The top-level language foregrounds AI, product thinking and production workflows. The visible social proof foregrounds brands. Neither by itself answers: “what changed because Miguel was there?”

**Why it matters:** Senior hiring decisions require impact and scope, not only technical relevance.

**Direction:** Promote quantified or concrete outcomes: time saved, errors reduced, workflows standardized, adoption, people trained, stakeholders aligned, assets/pages processed, cycle-time reduction, budget/effort impact, scale, or business decision enabled.

---

### POS-P1-05 — The final CTA reinforces maximum breadth instead of focus
**Severity:** P1  
**Confidence:** high

**Evidence:** Contact copy: “Any industry, any scale — if the challenge is right, let's talk.”

**Why it matters:** It is open and confident, but after an already broad positioning it tells the recruiter that Miguel is open to almost anything rather than reinforcing the type of problem he is best positioned to solve.

**Direction:** Later rewrite the CTA around the target problem/role family, while remaining open to sectors.

---

### POS-P1-06 — Brand credibility is not the same as personal ownership
**Severity:** P1  
**Confidence:** high

**Evidence:** Large client-logo section.

**Why it matters:** Logos answer “where has he worked?” but not “what did he personally own?” A recruiter can overestimate or underestimate contribution without context.

**Direction:** Keep selected logos as trust signals, but connect the strongest brands to specific case studies, role, scope and outcome.

## Positioning hypotheses to test against the market

These are hypotheses, not final recommendations. The final role should be chosen after CV/LinkedIn audit and a sample of real vacancies.

### Hypothesis A — AI / Innovation Project or Program Manager
Strong if evidence demonstrates cross-functional coordination, workflow transformation, adoption, stakeholder alignment and delivery ownership.

### Hypothesis B — Digital / Creative Technology Project Manager or Lead
Strong if the competitive advantage is the combination of deep production knowledge + automation/AI + ability to lead transformation in creative/marketing operations.

### Hypothesis C — Product / Operations profile in marketing technology or AI-enabled production
Plausible if the portfolio can prove problem discovery, prioritization, requirements, iteration, adoption, metrics and lifecycle ownership rather than only solution delivery.

### Hypothesis D — Technical Program / Delivery / Innovation Manager
Plausible if there is enough evidence of governance, dependencies, planning, people/stakeholder management and delivery at scale.

## What should NOT happen

- Do not hide the 3D/CGI foundation; use it as domain depth.
- Do not make “AI” the entire identity; AI should be a lever, not a substitute for a role.
- Do not list every project/tool equally.
- Do not invent management scale that cannot be evidenced.
- Do not choose a trendy title merely because it sounds more senior.

## Strongest perceived identity today

**Senior digital-production / creative-technology professional evolving toward AI-enabled workflow and systems transformation.**

This is credible, but it is still a descriptive identity rather than a sharply hireable category.

## Biggest positioning risk

**A recruiter sees a high-capability multidisciplinary profile but cannot confidently map it to a requisition in the first 30 seconds.**

## Priority conclusion

Before visual redesign, the audit must answer:

1. Which primary job family gives the strongest fit with Miguel's actual evidence?
2. Which 3–4 projects best prove that identity?
3. Which achievements prove ownership and business impact?
4. What should be de-emphasized so breadth reads as depth rather than noise?

Only after that should hero copy, case-study order and visual hierarchy be redesigned.
