# 04 — CV Audit

## Executive verdict

The CV has a strong underlying story but currently markets Miguel one level more broadly and strategically than the evidence consistently proves. The content is much stronger than a traditional creative/3D CV: it already shows AI adoption, process redesign, workshops, MVPs, project coordination and measurable operational impact. The main issue is not lack of substance; it is precision of positioning.

A recruiter can currently read at least four identities at once: AI transformation lead, digital innovation manager, project/program manager and senior creative-technology specialist. For a job search, the CV should make one of these the primary hiring category and use the others as supporting differentiators.

## P0 — Hiring category is still too broad

Current top line:

> AI & Digital Transformation Lead

Supporting line:

> GenAI Adoption | Operational Design | Workflow Optimization | Systems Thinking

This sounds senior and credible, but it can create an expectation of formal enterprise AI strategy/governance ownership that the rest of the CV only partially proves. The current Accenture role is described as `Assistant Manager | GenAI Adoption & Digital Innovation`, which is more grounded.

Recommendation: anchor the CV to a market-recognisable role family such as **AI / Digital Transformation Project Manager**, **AI & Digital Innovation Manager**, **AI Adoption / Transformation Manager**, or **Product Operations Manager** depending on the vacancy. Do not use one generic CV for all four families.

## P0 — Leadership and ownership need clearer evidence

The strongest evidence is present but spread across the document:

- coordinated multidisciplinary teams of up to 10 people;
- facilitated strategic workshops;
- translated operational needs into MVPs and internal tools;
- founder experience with direct client ownership, scope, quality and delivery;
- AI enablement/training;
- measurable operational improvements.

This should be surfaced earlier and written as ownership, not only participation. The BlackSheepStudio period is especially important because it proves direct client management and end-to-end responsibility — two things that are less visible in the current Accenture role.

## P1 — Strong metrics, but attribution must stay defensible

The CV states contributions to:

- 25% reduction in operational costs;
- 40% acceleration in time-to-market;
- productivity increases up to 40%.

These are valuable differentiators. Keep them only where Miguel can explain the baseline, initiative and his contribution in an interview. Avoid presenting them as enterprise-wide outcomes if they came from specific workflows/projects.

## P1 — Competency list is too broad

The `CORE COMPETENCIES` section contains more than twenty terms spanning AI strategy, transformation, automation, systems design, stakeholder management, program coordination, QA, estimation, innovation, Agile and Scrum.

This is ATS-friendly but weakens positioning. A tailored CV should normally foreground 8–12 competencies that map directly to the target role. For example, a transformation/project version could prioritize:

- Digital Transformation
- GenAI Adoption
- Project / Program Delivery
- Process & Workflow Optimization
- Stakeholder Management
- Cross-functional Leadership
- Workshop Facilitation / Discovery
- MVP Definition
- Change / Capability Enablement
- Operational Improvement

`AI Strategy` should be used carefully unless the target role accepts strategy at use-case/adoption level rather than enterprise governance level.

## P1 — Current tools section pulls the reader back toward execution

The CV still lists ComfyUI, Stable Diffusion, Flux, Unreal Engine, Unity, Figma, Adobe Creative Suite and other production tools. They are credible and useful differentiators, but a recruiter for transformation/management roles may interpret the profile as a senior technical maker rather than a manager who understands production.

Recommendation: split into `Management / Delivery` and `Technical / AI exposure`, or reduce the old execution tooling. The tools must support the target narrative rather than define it.

## P1 — Selected initiatives should show problem → role → outcome

The projects are relevant but mostly described as what the solution is. Rewrite them so each one answers:

1. What business/operational problem existed?
2. What did Miguel personally lead/define/build/facilitate?
3. What changed or what evidence of value exists?

The Radisson workshop is already close because it includes 22 stakeholders. QA and Estimation need equally concrete context and proof.

## P1 — Product positioning must remain precise

The CV includes Product Management training and product-like work, but Miguel has not held a formal Product Manager role. Therefore a generic `Product Manager` headline would currently be weaker than transformation, innovation, project or product-operations positioning.

`Product Operations`, `Innovation`, `MVP / Discovery` and `AI-enabled workflow transformation` are more defensible bridges into product-oriented roles.

## P1 — English is a real screening constraint for some target jobs

The CV correctly states `English B2 (daily use in international environments)`. This is truthful and should remain so. Several relevant manager/transformation vacancies ask for C1, so English is not a wording problem but a genuine market gap.

Possible wording if accurate:

> English B2 — daily professional use in international environments; currently progressing toward C1.

Do not claim C1 before it is true.

## P2 — Formatting / quality defects

Confirmed parsed-text defects in the current PDF:

- `Environment / tools: Environment / tools:` is duplicated.
- One section break merges `goals.• Collaborated` in the parsed PDF text.

These may look minor visually but matter for ATS parsing and signal insufficient final QA. The final exported PDF should be checked both visually and as extracted text.

## P2 — 20+ years is both an asset and a positioning risk

The long experience supports seniority and breadth, but leading with `20+ years` can make Miguel look overqualified for some mid-manager roles while not automatically proving formal transformation leadership.

Recommendation: keep the history but frame it as progression:

> 20+ years across digital production, experience design and technology, with recent years focused on AI-enabled transformation, operational improvement and cross-functional delivery.

That explains why the breadth matters.

## What is already strong

- Current `Assistant Manager` level is visible.
- AI adoption is connected to operational needs rather than framed as experimentation only.
- Cross-functional coordination and workshops are present.
- Founder history proves client-facing ownership.
- Certifications support the move toward Project/Product/Transformation.
- The CV contains concrete initiatives, not only generic responsibilities.
- Metrics exist.

## Recommended CV architecture

1. Name
2. One market-facing target title
3. 3–4 line executive summary
4. 8–12 target competencies
5. Current role with 5–6 evidence-based bullets
6. Earlier Accenture role
7. Founder role
8. Compressed previous creative/technical career
9. Selected transformation / AI initiatives
10. Certifications
11. Languages
12. Condensed tools

## Overall assessment

**Current strategic quality: 7.5/10**

The CV is already credible for conversations around digital transformation, AI adoption and operational innovation. To reach a stronger recruiter conversion level, the priority is not adding more achievements; it is making the target role unmistakable, tightening the competency/tooling surface and converting projects into evidence of leadership and business impact.
