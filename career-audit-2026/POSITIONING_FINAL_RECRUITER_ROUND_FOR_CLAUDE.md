# Final Recruiter Round — Find the Real Job Family

This is the last positioning round. Do not optimize for sounding modern, senior, innovative, or impressive. Optimize for **recognition, credibility, market reality, and recruiter conversion**.

## Core objective

We want to answer one practical question:

> **If you were a recruiter or hiring manager at a real company and you needed someone who genuinely does what Miguel can do, what job family/title would you search for? What headline would make you stop and think: “This person does what I need, and I trust the claim enough to call him”?**

The answer must be a **real, existing market role**, not a constructed positioning phrase.

## Non-negotiable principles

1. **No invented titles.** If the exact title is not observed in real job postings, do not recommend it as the primary identity.
2. **No hype.** Do not use AI, transformation, innovation, digital, creative technology, etc. merely because they sound current.
3. **No problem-dependent identity.** The role should not exist only when a company has one special temporary problem such as “we need a digital transformation,” “we need to adopt AI,” or “we need to improve a broken process.”
4. **No overly narrow domain trap.** The title should not unnecessarily restrict Miguel to only agencies, 3D, CGI, creative production, or marketing if his transferable skills support broader employers.
5. **No false breadth.** A title that sounds broad but actually routes recruiters toward IT operations, logistics, supply chain, ecommerce, ERP, engineering, or another function Miguel does not own is worse than a narrower but accurate title.
6. **The title must be understood immediately by HR.** A recruiter should know what kind of person this is, what kinds of responsibilities they normally own, and why a company would employ one as part of normal day-to-day business.
7. **Current evidence before aspiration.** Miguel wants growth toward management, ownership, projects, stakeholders and business, but the title must still be defensible from his current and past evidence.
8. **Use market evidence, not semantic elegance.** Search live Spain roles first; use Europe only as supporting evidence. Distinguish live openings from archetypes/closed roles.

## The professional service we are actually trying to name

Do NOT reduce Miguel to “improves processes.” That is only one part of his value.

The broader service is:

> **He helps digital/technology-driven work run, deliver and evolve. He can keep what already works operating reliably, coordinate people and stakeholders, manage delivery, respond to new needs, improve workflows and processes when required, and help create or implement new solutions, tools or ways of working when the business needs them.**

His value spans four modes:

1. **Run / operate what already works** — keep delivery, quality, coordination and workflows functioning.
2. **Deliver** — coordinate projects/initiatives, people, dependencies, stakeholders and outcomes.
3. **Improve / evolve** — identify friction, redesign workflows, standardize, optimize quality/efficiency/scalability.
4. **Create / implement new** — turn business needs into solutions, MVPs, tools, automation, AI-enabled workflows or new operating models.

AI, automation and digital transformation are **capabilities or contexts**, not necessarily the profession itself.

3D/CGI, XR, digital production and creative technology are **deep background and differentiating evidence**, not automatically the job title.

## Critical recruiter thought experiment

For every candidate title, answer these questions from the recruiter side:

1. **What business need makes me open this requisition?**
2. **Would I still hire this role if the company is already digital, AI-enabled, and its processes are working reasonably well?**
3. **What does this person own on an ordinary Tuesday?**
4. **What would I type into LinkedIn Recruiter / an ATS search?**
5. **Would Miguel appear credible next to the normal candidates for that title?**
6. **Would the title make me wrongly expect something he does not do?**
7. **Does the title cover run + delivery + improvement + creation, or only one of those four?**
8. **Does the title leave room for his 3D/CGI/production/AI background to be an advantage rather than a cage?**
